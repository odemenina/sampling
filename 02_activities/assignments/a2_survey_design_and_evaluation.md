1. Sample Type for the Canadian General Social Survey is Stratified Random Sampling.
 This method involves dividing the population into subgroups (strata) based on characteristics such as age, gender, and region, and then randomly selecting samples from each subgroup to ensure representation. The survey uses a cross-sectional design, which means it collects data from a sample of the population at one specific point in time.
•  The ten provinces of the target population are divided into strata (i.e., geographic areas).
•  Many of the Census Metropolitan Areas (CMAs) are each considered separate strata. This includes cities like St. John's, Halifax, Saint John, Montreal, Quebec City, Toronto, Ottawa (Ontario part of Ottawa - Gatineau CMA), Hamilton, Winnipeg, Regina, Saskatoon, Calgary, Edmonton, Vancouver, and Victoria.
•  CMAs not listed individually are grouped within their respective provinces. For example, Quebec, Ontario, and British Columbia have additional strata formed by grouping the remaining CMAs.
•  Non-CMA areas of each of the ten provinces are grouped to form ten additional strata. Moncton is included with the non-CMA group for New Brunswick. This results in a total of 27 strata.



2. Sample size
The sample size for the Canadian General Social Survey  is as follows:
•  Field Sample: Approximately 50,000 units were used in the field sample.
•  Invitation Letters: About 40,000 invitation letters were sent out to selected households across Canada.
•  Expected Completion: A completion of 24,000 questionnaires was expected.
This sample size was designed to ensure that the survey results would be representative of the target population, providing reliable and accurate data on giving, volunteering, and participating behaviors among Canadians.


3. Target population
All persons aged 15 years and older living in private households in the ten provinces of Canada: This excludes residents of the Yukon, Northwest Territories, and Nunavut, as well as full-time residents of institutions.
•  Exclusions: The survey excluded full-time residents of institutions (those residing in institutions for more than six months).
•  Geographic Coverage: The survey aimed to capture a wide range of experiences and behaviors from individuals across various regions and demographics within the ten provinces



4. Sampling frame
The sampling frame includes a comprehensive list of households in the ten provinces of Canada, which was used to select survey participants. This ensures a broad and representative sample.
Data Sources specified:
•  Household lists derived from the Canadian Census provide a foundational base for the sampling frame.
•  Various administrative data sources are incorporated to enhance the frame.
•  Statistics Canada's dwelling frame is used to further refine the selection.
Also  the next combination of Telephone Numbers includung:
•  Traditional landline telephone numbers are included in the frame.
•  Cellular telephone numbers are also incorporated to ensure coverage of households that primarily use mobile phones.
•  These telephone numbers are combined with address information to create a unified sampling frame, allowing for better coverage and accuracy in selecting survey participants.



5. Survey mode(s) 
Data for the survey was collected using two primary modes: an electronic questionnaire and computer-assisted telephone interviewing (CATI).
•  Electronic Questionnaire: Respondents could choose to complete the survey online, which provided a convenient and accessible way to participate.
•  CATI (Computer-Assisted Telephone Interviewing): Interviewers conducted telephone interviews using a computer system to record responses directly into a database. This method ensured accuracy and efficiency in data collection.
•  Language Options: The survey was available in both English and French to accommodate respondents' language preferences.
•  Average Completion Time: The average time to complete the survey was estimated to be 44 minutes.



6. Timeline
The survey was conducted over a four-month period to gather timely and relevant data. 
The data collection period was from September 4, 2018, to December 28, 2018




7. Response rate
The overall response rate for the survey was 41.9%.
The response rate is influenced by various factors such as survey mode, length of the questionnaire, and respondents' willingness to participate. 
Efforts were made to encourage participation and minimize non-response.




8. Weights
Using weights in survey analysis is essential to:
•	Produce unbiased and accurate estimates of the target population.
•	Adjust for survey design features, such as stratification and unequal probabilities of selection.
•	Correct for non-response and coverage errors, minimizing potential biases.
Weights were applied to the data to ensure that the results are representative of the entire population. This helps to adjust for any over- or under-representation of certain groups.
A weighting factor (WGHT_PER) is available on the microdata file for analysis at the person level. 
Bootstrap weights were also created for design-based variance estimation

10. Cleaning, imputation, etc.
Cleaning and imputation for the Canadian General Social Survey are critical processes to ensure the data's accuracy and completeness.

Data Cleaning:
•	Automatic and Manual Edits: Edits were performed both automatically and manually at various stages of processing. These included:
•	Family Edits: Checking family relationships to ensure the integrity of matrix data.
•	Consistency Edits: Ensuring the survey data matched expected patterns, such as verifying respondent age against the birth date.
•	Flow Edits: Ensuring respondents followed the correct path through the survey and fixing off-path situations.
•	Error Detection During Interviews: The CATI system (Computer-Assisted Telephone Interviewing) had built-in edits to automatically follow the flow of the 	questionnaire and identify out-of-range values.
•	Errors detected by the CATI system were resolved immediately with the respondent or forwarded to head office for resolution if necessary.
•	Head Office Edits: Involved more detailed edits compared to the CATI system, ensuring all interviewer comments were reviewed and taken into account.

Imputation
•	Donor Imputation: Missing data filled using similar (donor) records, identified through matching characteristics.
•	Mean Imputation: Used when donor imputation was not possible, applying average values from a pool of donors.
•	Income Data: Personal income linked from tax data (T1FF) for 81.9% of respondents, with missing data imputed. Family income used instead of household income, obtained for 81.7% of households, with imputation for missing data.




11. Sources of error
Sources of error for Canadian General Social Survey involved:
•	Potential sources of error include non-response bias (differences between respondents and non-respondents)
•	Measurement error (errors in how questions were understood or answered)
•	Sampling error (errors due to the sample not perfectly representing the population)
•	Non-sampling error (imperfect coverage, non-response, response errors, and processing errors)




12. Limitations, known biases, etc
Limitations, known biases for Canadian General Social Survey involved
•	Exclusion of Certain Populations (the survey excludes residents of the Yukon, Northwest Territories, and Nunavut, as well as full-time residents of institutions,   which may limit the generalizability of the results to these populations)
•	Sampling error (variations that arise because the survey results are based on a sample of the population rather than a full census)
•	Coverage error(differences between the target population and the surveyed population, such as households without telephones being excluded)
•	Response Errors(inaccuracies in the information provided by respondents)
•	Processing Errors(errors occurring during data processing)
•	Non-Response Error(occurs when selected households or individuals do not participate)
•	Quality Assurance Error (includes a well-tested questionnaire, proven methodology, specialized interviewers, and strict quality control)
